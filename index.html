Common bugs

  • Use mid = lo + (hi - lo) // 2 in fixed-width languages to avoid overflow.
  • Infinite loop when lo = mid and mid rounds down.

Behavioral

Build a bank of STAR stories. Pick a question, write your story, then time yourself saying it aloud (aim for about 2 minutes).

What interviewers listen for

  • Spend about 60% on Action. Keep Situation and Task short (about 20%) and finish with Result (about 20%).
  • Say "I", not "we". They are hiring you, not your team.
  • Put numbers on results: latency down 40%, saved 10 hours a week, unblocked 3 teams.
  • For failure questions, show what you learned and what you changed afterwards.
  • Prepare 6–8 strong stories that each fit several questions (conflict, ownership, failure, impact).
  • Amazon maps questions to its Leadership Principles. Google looks at "Googleyness" and leadership. Meta focuses on conflict, growth and impact.

AI & ML interviews

For AI engineer, ML engineer and applied scientist roles. Build intuition with the visual labs, practise ML coding from scratch, design AI systems, and drill the concepts.

AI engineer

Builds products on top of foundation models: retrieval, agents, evaluation, latency and cost.

Focus on

  • RAG and agent system design
  • LLM sampling, context, tokens and cost
  • Evaluating LLM output
  • Standard coding rounds (LeetCode medium)

ML engineer

Trains, ships and monitors models: features, pipelines, ranking, serving and drift.

Focus on

  • ML system design (recommendations, fraud)
  • ML coding from scratch in NumPy
  • Metrics, thresholds and experiments
  • Two coding rounds at SWE difficulty

Applied / research scientist

Goes deeper on modeling: optimization, architectures, experiment design and papers.

Focus on

  • Optimization and training dynamics
  • Transformers and attention in depth
  • Statistics and evaluation design
  • Discussing your past research in depth

What a typical loop looks like

  1. Recruiter call and coding screen. Usually a standard algorithms problem; some companies swap in an ML coding task.
  2. ML coding. Implement something from scratch in Python and NumPy: attention, k-means, logistic regression, sampling.
  3. ML fundamentals or depth. Bias and variance, metrics, optimization, transformers, and the details of your own projects.
  4. ML or LLM system design. "Design a recommendation feed", "Design a RAG assistant", "Design an agent that can take actions".
  5. Behavioral and project deep dive. A model you shipped, what went wrong in production, and how you measured impact.

Gradient descent

Each step moves against the gradient: w ← w − lr · ∇L(w). The learning rate decides how far.

Things to try

  • Narrow valley with plain descent: watch it zig-zag. Then switch to momentum.
  • Push the learning rate past about 0.5 on the narrow valley and it diverges.
  • Two valleys: start on either side of the middle and it lands in different minima.

Questions interviewers ask

  • Why does Adam often train faster than plain SGD?
  • How do you pick a learning rate? What does warmup do?
  • Why does SGD noise help find better minima?

How decoding works

TemperatureDivides the logits before the softmax. Below 1 sharpens the distribution; above 1 flattens it.
Top-kKeeps only the k most likely tokens.
Top-pKeeps the smallest set of top tokens whose probabilities add up to p (nucleus sampling).

Questions interviewers ask

  • Why can greedy decoding produce repetitive text?
  • When would you use temperature 0?
  • Why is top-p often preferred to a fixed top-k?
  • How does beam search differ from sampling?
